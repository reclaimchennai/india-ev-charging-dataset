Reality check

Near-parity with the official national count

The government counts stations across every operator, so no single app or network matches it. Stacking nine independent feeds closed the gap: the merged dataset stands at 28,553, about 98% of the mid-2025 official figure and above the April-2025 count, and exceeds the March-2024 figure in 29 of 34 states.

Only Karnataka and Delhi still trail

Tamil Nadu (2,254 vs 643), Uttar Pradesh, Rajasthan, Gujarat, Maharashtra and most others now exceed the March-2024 official count - real growth plus a fresher, multi-source snapshot. Karnataka (2,365 vs 5,130) and Delhi (819 vs 1,886) still trail because both run large state-utility (DISCOM) fleets; Karnataka closed much of its gap once BESCOM's own EV Mithra feed was added. Coverage grows by adding each operator's feed, not by crawling one harder.

Geography

A western and southern network

Maharashtra leads with 4,014 stations, then Karnataka, Tamil Nadu and Uttar Pradesh. The top eight states hold roughly two-thirds of every charger in the dataset - concentrated in the industrial west and the southern metros.

Per capita

Divide by people and the map inverts

On raw counts Uttar Pradesh looks well-served (4th, 2,402 stations). Against its 238 million people it drops to 10.1 per million - half the national average. Tiny Goa (138.3) and Kerala (51.4), modest in absolute terms, run the densest networks in the country. National average: 20.4 stations per million.

The real divide is the Gangetic plain

Bihar (3.3/M), Jharkhand (6.4), West Bengal (8.0) and Uttar Pradesh (10.1) hold ~505 million people between them - nearly 40% of India - yet sit far below the national average. The south and west - Goa, Kerala, Haryana, Telangana, Delhi, Karnataka - run 2–5× denser per head.

Speed, capacity & connectors

DC-led, built around CCS2

Among the ~14,000 stations with a published power rating, DC fast (25–60 kW) dominates; the remaining ~51% come from location-only feeds (Kia/Numocity, ChargeZone, Ather Grid) that don't publish power. CCS2 leads the connector mix, with Type 2 and 3-pin/15A sockets common on the newer lite-charger networks.

Tariffs

Two prices: ₹14 and the market

Across 10,429 priced stations the median is ₹21/kWh. But the distribution is bimodal: a wall of stations at exactly ₹14 - the subsidised oil-PSU tariff (HPCL and BPCL) - and a private-CPO market clustered at ₹21–26.

Operators

Three strategies on a 223-operator market

The leaders divide into oil PSUs (near-100% DC, flat ₹14, nationwide forecourts), conglomerates (Tata, Jio-bp, Adani - broad and premium) and pure-play CPOs (ChargeZone, Statiq, Bolt - the fastest-growing). Jio-bp packs the most guns per site; Bolt and EFi run the lite 3.3 kW two-wheeler networks. ChargeZone's station count is an upper bound - its B2C app aggregates roaming partners.

A competitive market, no monopoly

The top four operators run ~56% of stations, but the market stays competitive (national operator HHI ≈ 1,242 - still "moderately concentrated"). The leader flips by region and by segment: oil PSUs own the highways, pure-play CPOs the metros, and state utilities fill the gaps their own apps expose. 223 distinct operators appear in all.

Method & caveats

  • Nine sources. MG eHUB and BESCOM EV Mithra (operator / state-utility apps); OpenChargeMap and OpenStreetMap (open community data); MeeTicket (Telangana); and the ChargeZone and Kia/Numocity aggregator platforms plus Bolt.Earth and Ather Grid, each read from its own public map data.
  • Merge. 52,691 raw records collapsed to 28,553 by spatial dedup: within 150 m treated as one site when closer than 75 m or when name/operator agree; every non-null field and connector kept.
  • Cleaning. Junk power values nulled (ceiling 400 kW), test/commissioning units removed, and a handful of out-of-land coordinates relocated or dropped against India admin boundaries.
  • Validation-ready. Every one of the 28,553 stations carries coordinates; 99% an operator. State/district assigned by point-in-polygon against India admin boundaries.
  • Caveats. Power/connector detail is missing for ~51% (the Numocity, ChargeZone and Ather Grid feeds are location-only), so the speed and capacity figures describe only the ~14,000 stations that publish it. ChargeZone's total is inflated by B2C roaming aggregation. Counts are sites, not guns.
